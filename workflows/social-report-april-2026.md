**Generated:** June 5, 2026

**Platforms covered:** LinkedIn · YouTube · Instagram  
**Comparison period:** March 2026 (LinkedIn available from scraped data)

---

## Executive Summary

April was CXL's highest-volume publishing month across all three platforms in this dataset. LinkedIn hit 10 posts (2.4/week), YouTube published 5 videos including its first-ever Short, and Instagram posted 6 times with Reels appearing for the first time.

Volume went up. Average engagement per post went down.

LinkedIn averaged 19.9 interactions per post in April — only marginally ahead of March's 18.6 average — despite far more publishing effort. The top of the distribution was strong (two posts at 38 and 44 interactions), but the bottom dragged the average: three posts generated 5 interactions or fewer. Promotional content for the Jeff Sauer live course was the main dead weight, consuming 3 of 10 publishing slots for near-zero return.

The clearest signal: **Claude Code content was already the strongest theme in April**, before it became the headline story of May. The two Claude Code posts (Apr 15 and Apr 27) were the month's top performers. The AI Agents for B2B Marketing program launch (Apr 13) also outperformed when framed as insight rather than as a sales post.

YouTube's April output was n8n-heavy — all four long-form uploads were recordings from the March 30–April 3 n8n week. Best performer: "Build Your First AI Agent with n8n (step by step)" at 323 views. No LinkedIn posts promoted any of the April YouTube uploads directly, making this the only month where YouTube and LinkedIn were effectively operating as separate channels.

Instagram posted Reels for the first time (3 Reels out of 6 posts), but the format alone didn't move the needle. Captions remained verbatim LinkedIn copy, Reels underperformed carousels, and the account generated only 1 comment across the entire month.

### Top Performing Patterns

**1. Claude Code as a systems story, not a product announcement**  
The two best posts of the month shared a structure: they described a specific marketing system someone could build, then named Claude Code as the tool enabling it. Neither led with "attend our webinar."

- **Apr 27** (44 interactions): Described building a context engine, synthetic focus groups, and live data integrations — then named the instructors. Framed as "what AI systems you'll be able to build," not "join us on May 7th."  
- **Apr 15** (38 interactions): Opened with audience observation ("You've probably seen a wave of posts about Claude Code lately"), created relevance before the ask, and listed concrete live demo deliverables. 4 shares — the "I'm sending this to my team" signal.

Both treated Claude Code content as practitioner education, not event marketing. The framing carried both posts.

**2. Tool demos with a built-in reason to share**  
"Analyze competitor search ads at scale" (25) and "Who's stealing your clicks?" (19) both showed a specific workflow someone could replicate — and both included a live tool to try. The competitor ads post got 4 shares (tied for the month's most), directly from the "I need to send this to someone running ads" response.

The pattern: **show the system + give them access** outperforms showing the system alone.

**3. Program launches work when framed as market insight**  
The AI Agents program launch (Apr 13, 28 interactions) opened with a diagnosis: "AI is moving fast, and most teams are still stuck deciding which tools to use." The program was the answer to that diagnosis. Four comments and two shares suggest it resonated as a point of view, not as an ad.

### Weak Performance Patterns

**1. Live course promotion is the lowest-ROI content type**  
Three posts promoted the Jeff Sauer live course (Apr 8: 3 interactions, Apr 20: 5 interactions, plus a March 18 post at 9). They consumed 30% of April's publishing capacity and generated 8% of its interactions. The content gave no insight, no unique angle, no reason to engage — only a CTA and a date. LinkedIn's algorithm correctly buried them.

**2. Event countdowns without social proof fall flat**  
"n8n week is halfway through" (Apr 1, 7 interactions) read as an internal update, not a content post. No takeaway, no reason for a non-attendee to engage. Same pattern as the May pricing reminder — event logistics dressed as content.

### Key Observations

**CXL published its first YouTube Short in April**  
"Is AI causing you to lose your tone of voice?" (28 seconds) was uploaded April 7 alongside the matching LinkedIn post and Instagram carousel. It generated 50 views — well below any long-form video. This was a first attempt, and the underperformance is more diagnostic than damning: Shorts require a different hook structure, title format, and thumbnail strategy than long-form. Publishing it was the right call; the execution needs to evolve.

**The n8n first agent video outperformed by 2× despite no LinkedIn amplification**  
"Build Your First AI Agent with n8n (step by step)" (323 views) was the best-performing April video — and it got there without a LinkedIn post promoting it. The Apr 14 LinkedIn post was about competitor search ads, not the n8n video. YouTube's own algorithm drove the views, likely because "step by step" + "first AI agent" signals high relevance for beginners actively searching the topic.

This is the only evidence in this dataset of YouTube views coming entirely from YouTube-native discovery rather than LinkedIn referral.

**All long-form April content underperforms the channel average**  
173, 141, and 139 views are well below the channel lifetime average of ~1,338. This is consistent with May's pattern for non-Claude-Code content. n8n webinar recordings face a higher watch-time bar (48–60 minutes) and compete in a more crowded instructional space. Title optimization — specifically adding "step by step," "tutorial," or specific outcome language — appears to be the difference between 173 and 323 views within the same month.

**No LinkedIn → YouTube cross-promotion in April**  
In May, the Claude Code video (269 views) was directly promoted via a matching LinkedIn post the same day. In April, zero LinkedIn posts pointed to any YouTube upload. The n8n webinar recordings appeared on YouTube with no corresponding LinkedIn post. The gap in views between April's long-form average (194) and May's best video (269) is at least partially explained by this missing amplification.

**Caption strategy remains unchanged**  
All six April posts used verbatim LinkedIn captions — including long multi-paragraph text blocks designed for LinkedIn's text-first feed. Instagram posts are consumed in a completely different context: fast-scroll, mobile-first, visual-primary. The same caption that reads as educational on LinkedIn reads as a wall of text on Instagram. No hashtags are visible in any caption, further limiting discoverability.

## Cross-Platform Insights

**Claude Code was already the dominant content theme — two months before it peaked**  
April's two best LinkedIn posts (38 and 44 interactions) were both Claude Code posts. May's best LinkedIn post (58 interactions) and best YouTube video (269 views, 4 comments) were also Claude Code. The April data shows this wasn't luck — the theme was already tested and winning before CXL made it the center of May's distribution.

**April was n8n month on YouTube, but LinkedIn didn't know it**  
Four of five April YouTube uploads were n8n week recordings. None had a LinkedIn post pointing to them. YouTube and LinkedIn operated in parallel, not in series. May demonstrated (Claude Code video, 269 views) that LinkedIn amplification doubles YouTube performance. April left that multiplier on the table for every n8n upload.

**First 3-platform publishing day produced no measurable amplification**  
April 7 was the first day CXL published on all three platforms simultaneously (LinkedIn + YouTube Short + Instagram Carousel — all about the tone of voice tool). Total April 7 performance: LinkedIn 18 interactions, YouTube Short 50 views, Instagram 3 likes. Three-platform days aren't inherently more effective — the content on each platform still needs to be built for that platform's distribution logic.

**April 14 was the closest to a true coordinated day**  
The Apr 14 n8n video (323 views) and the Apr 14 LinkedIn competitor ads post (25 interactions) and the Apr 14 Instagram Reel (3 likes) were all published the same day — but the LinkedIn and Instagram content promoted the competitor ads tool, while YouTube uploaded the n8n video. No cross-amplification was possible because the content wasn't aligned. The 323 YouTube views came from YouTube's own algorithm.

## Content Opportunities

**1. Build a Claude Code content series before the competition catches up**  
Two Claude Code posts outperformed everything else in April. By May, Claude Code content became CXL's single strongest theme across both active platforms. April shows the window was already open — and it's still open now. The next angles: Cursor for marketers, `.cursorrules`-style brand briefs, Claude Code + Ahrefs workflows, Claude Code + GA4 MCP.

**2. Always pair April-style YouTube uploads with matching LinkedIn posts**  
The n8n week videos generated 826 views with zero LinkedIn promotion. May proved LinkedIn drives YouTube views. The missed amplification in April represents a replicable playbook for any future webinar recording uploads: publish the YouTube video → publish a LinkedIn post about the same content the same day.

**3. Stop producing dry live course promotion posts**  
Apr 8 (3 interactions) and Apr 20 (5 interactions) are two of the three lowest-performing LinkedIn posts in the April–May dataset. Course promotion works when framed as insight (Apr 13 program launch: 28 interactions). It fails completely when framed as logistics. Every course promotion post should answer: "what will someone be able to do after this that they can't do now?" Lead with that answer.

**4. Optimize YouTube titles for search intent**  
"Build Your First AI Agent with n8n (step by step)" (323 views) significantly outperformed the other three n8n uploads with nearly identical content type. The title contains a specific audience signal (first AI agent), a format signal (step by step), and a named tool (n8n). The other titles — "Build a content automation system," "This n8n workflow saves hours" — are weaker on all three dimensions. Standardizing title structure could bring the underperforming videos closer to 323 views.

**5. Build Reels natively, not from LinkedIn repurposing**  
April's three Reels were the right format move, but the wrong execution. A Reel built for Instagram starts with a visual hook in the first 2 seconds, uses a truncated caption (150 characters max before "More"), and is structured around a single idea. The Reels published in April were clips of LinkedIn content — the hook wasn't designed for Reels, and the caption wasn't adapted for mobile. The next step is building one Reel from scratch for Instagram, not repurposing from LinkedIn.

**6. Treat the Apr 13 program launch as the template for future announcements**  
It's the only promotional post in April that performed above average. Why: it opened with a diagnosis of market reality ("AI is moving fast and most teams are stuck"), positioned the product as the answer, and gave specific credibility signals (7 courses, 12 live sessions). This is the formula. Every future launch post should follow the same structure: diagnosis → solution → specific proof.
