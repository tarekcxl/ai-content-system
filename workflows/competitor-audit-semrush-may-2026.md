## Executive Summary

Semrush operates at a fundamentally different content velocity and platform maturity than CXL across all three channels. In May 2026, Semrush published 23 LinkedIn posts (vs. CXL's 6), 18 Instagram posts (vs. CXL's 4), and 5 YouTube videos (vs. CXL's 2). The output gap is the largest single structural difference.

But volume isn't the whole story. Semrush runs a distinct content playbook on each platform — cultural/humor carousels with minimal copy on LinkedIn, native Reels and tactical carousels on Instagram, and short-form search-optimized educational videos on YouTube. None of these mirror CXL's approach, and all three outperform CXL's equivalents.

The clearest CXL opportunity: Semrush does not publish original data or research content. CXL's "We analyzed X to understand Y" formula (AI FOMO Reddit analysis, job openings analysis) is a content pillar Semrush doesn't own.

### Top Performing Patterns

**1. Minimal-copy + document carries the message**
Semrush's most reliable format: 1–5 word ironic caption + high-effort carousel document. "Met Gala but make it Marketing" (523), "just checking" (310), "we're almost halfway team" (378), "truly life changing" (252), "summer in marketing" (294). These are consistently top performers and share a structural logic: the cultural hook creates curiosity, the document delivers the payoff. The low-copy approach signals confidence.

**2. Real-time news synthesis with consequence framing**
Both top commentary posts frame news as downstream consequence: "Google is now pulling Reddit threads into Business Profiles → here's what that means for your brand" (500 interactions, 53 shares — the highest share count of the month). Google I/O analysis (373, 34 shares). The framing isn't "here's what happened" — it's "here's what you'll lose if you don't act."

**3. Milestone/cultural moments anchor the highest peaks**
The 17-year anniversary post (1,361 interactions) was the single best-performing post of the month by 2.7×. Milestones and cultural calendar moments (Met Gala, halfway through the year) outperform product posts and tactical content.

**4. Product launches with genuine narrative**
Semrush x Lovable (244) and AI agents/crawl post (181) both frame a product feature as a problem the audience didn't know they had. The Lovable post in particular ("fast has never meant findable — until now") names the tension before the solution.

### Weak Patterns

**Promotional posts underperform universally**
The Cannes ad-cost post (14), "Hot take: Agentic Search" (46), and event promo posts (56–79) consistently land at the bottom. Semrush has the same promotional content problem as CXL: overt CTAs and event announcements are deprioritized by the algorithm and the audience.

## Differentiation Opportunities for CXL

**1. Own the original research/data content pillar — Semrush doesn't**
Semrush publishes news synthesis and tactical how-tos. They do not publish "We analyzed X to understand Y" original research posts. CXL's AI FOMO Reddit analysis and B2B marketer survey are a format Semrush hasn't touched. A recurring "CXL Research:" series (monthly dataset + narrative) would be genuinely uncontested territory in this space.

**2. The AI systems content angle is CXL's native territory**
Semrush covers AI visibility, AI crawlability, and agentic search — all at the tool/visibility level. CXL covers AI systems for practitioners: Claude Code for marketers, n8n workflows, cursor briefs. These are different layers of the same trend. Semrush owns the "is your brand findable by AI?" angle. CXL can own "here's how marketers actually build with AI."

**3. Practitioner-level specificity vs. platform-level analysis**
Semrush posts tend to end with a link to a Semrush tool. CXL posts can end with a usable framework. The "copyable system" format (GitHub for marketers: 78 interactions) outperforms everything else in CXL's May data and is structurally different from anything Semrush publishes.
