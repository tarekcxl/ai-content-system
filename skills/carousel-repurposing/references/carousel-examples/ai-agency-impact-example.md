Title: How AI is changing client expectations for agencies, and how agencies are adapting

AI's impact on marketing teams has been talked about a lot.
Will it replace jobs? Support teams? Shrink them?
But most of that conversation focuses on in-house teams.
What hasn't really been discussed is what this means for agencies.
In this edition, we spoke with marketing leaders and agency teams to understand how AI is actually reshaping agency work, and what that means for you if you're part of one.
How AI is changing what clients expect from agencies
We asked CMOs and VP-level marketers on how they're working with agencies today.
A few patterns stood out:

1. AI capability is now a selection criteria
20% of marketing leaders said an agency's AI strategy is already a factor in how they vet and select partners.
It's no longer a 'nice to have.' It's becoming a basic expectation and one of the few ways agencies can differentiate.
"Now when I'm evaluating marketing agencies, a significant factor is how they use AI to speed up the production process and make it more efficient."


2. Speed is no longer negotiable
40% expect agencies to move faster or deliver better results in less time.
The assumption is that if AI can speed things up, timelines should follow.
Long onboarding phases and slow research cycles are harder to justify.
"They should be able to work at a much faster rate with a more consistent output as they can ingest massive amounts of information that would previously require a long ramp time."
3. Some work is already moving in-house
27% of respondents specifically mentioned replacing parts of agency work, especially copywriting and basic production, with internal AI tools or 'agents.'
"My first consideration is whether I can build an agent in Claude to do the work a marketing agency would have initially done. If not, I'll consider the agency."


4. More demand for strategy, less for execution
At the same time, 20% are shifting spend toward higher-level work, things like positioning, creative direction, and domain expertise.
Less execution. More strategy.
"Agencies will spend less time doing production work… and more time providing things that AI is bad at, such as domain expertise, design taste, etc."

What this means
Clients aren’t just evaluating agencies anymore. They’re evaluating whether they need one at all.
Execution is being commoditized. Speed is expected. And AI is becoming the baseline.
Which means differentiation is shifting away from: “we can do this for you” →  “we know what actually matters and what to do next”.
What to do now
Stop positioning around execution (SEO, ads, content, etc.).
Start positioning around decisions, strategy, and outcomes.
Make your AI capability visible, but don’t lead with tools, lead with impact.

The challenges agencies are facing right now
To understand how agencies themselves are dealing with this shift, we also looked at responses from agency leaders.
Here's what stood out:
1. The risk of becoming less relevant
33% of agency leaders are concerned that as AI tools improve, and get cheaper, the value of 'high-touch' agency services will decline.
If AI tools can deliver faster and at a fraction of the cost, clients will start questioning what they're actually paying for.
"Will AI-related software become so good…at a super low cost…to the point where our high-touch delivery will not matter?"
2. The internal shift isn't smooth
AI adoption inside agencies isn't as straightforward as it sounds. There's a mix of tool overload and resistance from teams, especially when AI is seen as a threat rather than a tool. 
"My employees are resistant to AI at first because they feel like it is a replacement of their job."
"It's not always clear which tools to focus on…It's honestly a bit overwhelming."
What this means
The risk isn’t just external. It’s internal, agencies are trying to adopt AI while: teams are resistant, tools are overwhelming, and positioning hasn’t caught up yet.
What to do now
Upskill teams around workflows, not just tools.
Simplify your stack (less tools, more systems).

What to do now


AI is making execution faster and cheaper. Which makes it harder to charge for. So the value has to move upstream.
Into:
Defining the right problem
Shaping strategy and direction
Designing systems and workflows that scale
Making decisions on what to prioritize and what to ignore
Interpreting and improving AI output, not just generating it
This is where agencies become harder to replace.
