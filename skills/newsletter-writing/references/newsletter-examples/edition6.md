Title: Is AEO/GEO just a buzzword?

Reddit has become one of the most valuable places to understand what your audience are actually talking about. So we did what any curious marketer would do, we went subreddit-hopping. 

From marketing subreddits to SEO discussions, we were looking for real conversations, real opinions, and real pain points to help us shape this newsletter.

Eventually, we stumbled on a thread in r/SEO with a bold claim:

“AEO, GEO is a bullshit buzzword, intended to trick clients. Change my mind.”



The comments were split. Some call AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) nonsense. Others defend it. So in this edition, we’re breaking down that thread and adding our take.

First things first, the author is right about one thing: SEO fundamentals aren’t going anywheredead. Valuable content still wins. Backlinks still count. Authority still matters.

But if you stop there and don’t adapt to how people find information today, you will fall behind. AEO and GEO don’t replace SEO. They layer on top of it.




The shift in backlink value


One Reddit user agrees with the author and says they can’t see how AEO/GEO meaningfully differs from traditional SEO practices.


And at first glance, that’s a fair reaction. Traditional SEO is built on backlinks as proof of authority. GEO, on the other hand, builds authority through brand presence across the web.

To illustrate why this difference matters, Ahrefs ran an experiment. They created a fictional luxury brand called Xarumei, launched an official website with the “truth,” and then planted detailed fake stories across Reddit and Medium.


What happened next?
AI models like Gemini and ChatGPT ignored the official website and confidently repeated the fabricated backstory.

The takeaway: for LLMs, repetition and narrative richness can outweigh source-of-truth authority.

Traditional SEO: You earn backlinks to establish your site as a trusted source. 
AEO/GEO: You generate widespread brand mentions to create recognition and association.




Focusing on citations and the sentiment behind them is definitely a best practice that addresses the question above.

When an LLM repeatedly sees your brand mentioned in “best of” discussions alongside established players, even without links, it starts grouping you with them.

This isn’t just SEO.
It’s digital PR.

Top cited domains



If AEO/GEO and SEO were the same, the sites ranking #1 on Google would be the ones the AI always quotes. 
They aren't.

And this user agrees with this statement.



An Semrush study found that ChatGPT frequently cites pages that are buried in position 21 or lower on Google.





Traditional SEO: If you’re not on page one, your chances of being seen are low.
AEO/GEO: Even if you’re not on page one, if your content provides a more direct and structured answer than the top result, AI will surface it.


This happens because AI models use a technique called "Query Fan-out." 
When you ask a question, the AI doesn't just run one search; it runs several variations of your question in the background. It then looks for consensus across many different sites.

We noticed this firsthand while building our Chrome extension that extracts the queries ChatGPT uses.




So for example, when we asked: “What is the best SEO keyword research tool?”, these were the searches triggered by chatGPT:

“Best SEO keyword research tools 2025”.
“SEO keyword research tool comparison features pricing”.

Because of this, a small niche blog with a perfectly structured “how-to” table can get cited over a large corporate site with thousands of backlinks that bury the answer under 2,000 words of fluff.

Loss of traffic



Another Reddit user highlighted a point we’ve touched on before: if you rely only on traditional SEO and ignore AIO and GEO, you’ll lose out on traffic.

Ahrefs’ latest study shows AI Overviews appearing on 21% of keywords, with informational searches driving nearly all of them.




This means relying only on “how-to” and “what-if” articles is risky, because Google’s AI summaries are now answering those questions directly.

So two things need to happen.
First, identify the gap between your content and the AI summary, then optimize to increase your chances of being mentioned. Here’s a quick video to get you started.

Second, double down on pages that AI Overviews don’t typically trigger, like comparison and pricing pages.

Traditional SEO: You write "What-if" and "How-to" articles to win clicks and build your top-of-funnel traffic.
AEO/GEO: You analyze the gap between your content and the AI summary. Instead of just writing a guide, you optimize for citations within the AI answer itself so your brand is mentioned even if the user never visits the page.


Is AEO/GEO really a buzzword?

Yes, "AEO/GEO" is a buzzword, but the shift it describes is real.


The Reddit skeptics are right to question the hype, but the data tells a clear story.
If you’re not optimizing for AI extraction, managing brand mentions, and structuring content for direct answers, even a #1 ranking may not be enough because you’ll lose AI visibility.
