Key insights


Specialized roles in Growth & Performance and CRO deliver strong career returns, offering above-average salaries and facing less applicant competition than most other marketing positions.

General Marketing and Social Media roles remain the most common entry points into the field. However, they also attract the highest number of applicants per opening, which makes these positions significantly more competitive.


We compared our findings with the 2025 Exit Five B2B Marketing Salary Report. When you look at both side-by-side, a few things stand out:




Exit Five found that Product Marketing is the highest-paying role at $146,000. However, our analysis shows these jobs are much rarer than Social Media or General Marketing roles. It’s a good career path, but there are fewer open doors at the moment.


Exit Five shows “Digital & Performance” roles averaging $105K, while “Growth” roles average $132K. In our 1,000-job dataset, companies are also hiring far more Growth titles. There’s significant overlap in skills and responsibilities between these roles. However, Growth is framed as revenue ownership, Digital as channel execution and compensation follows perception.


What this means for you

The hiring market isn’t just showing us which roles are in demand, it’s showing us how competitive marketing has become. Yes, some roles attract more applicants than others. Yes, some roles pay more than others. 

But the outcome is always the same: no matter which sub-domain you’re in, you have to stand out.

In high-volume roles like Social Media and General Marketing, standing out means building a strong portfolio, staying on top of fast-changing trends, and using AI tools to improve the quality and speed of your work.

In more specialized roles like CRO, Growth, and Performance, standing out means going deeper into experimentation, analytics, and data-driven decision-making and being able to show real results, not just talk about them.

So while this data gives us useful direction, marketing is competitive across the board. And the best way to stay ahead is to keep learning, keep testing, and keep upskilling. This is the time to do it.

Key insights

In 4 out of every 10 jobs in this list, the employer explicitly mentions AI. 

While tools like ChatGPT get the most hype, Automation is mentioned in 11.3% of all jobs which is nearly 4x more often than specific tools like ChatGPT. 

SEO and Content roles has the highest "Generative AI" focus at 28.8%, confirming it is the main domain where AI is used for creating work.


AI is mentioned in nearly 60% of executive-level roles but only 29% of entry-level roles. This suggests that companies are looking for leaders to build AI strategies, rather than just juniors to use AI tools.




What this means for you

Nearly half of marketing job listings now mention AI. That means AI skills are quickly becoming a baseline expectation. 

So the most practical way to build an advantage is to focus on real use cases inside your domain.

Start by mapping AI to the work you already do:

If you work in content or SEO, experiment with generative AI for drafts, outlines, and content optimization.
If you work in growth or performance, test AI for ad variations, audience research, and automated reporting.
If you work in CRM or lifecycle, explore AI for segmentation, personalization, and message testing.

Next, connect these tools into simple workflows. 

The data shows automation is mentioned far more often than specific AI tools meaning companies value marketers who can make systems run, not just use standalone apps.

Finally, turn one working use case into a repeatable process and document it.

The future of recruitment

One interesting find: 44 job listings now disclose that AI may play a role in screening and processing applications.


These systems typically review resumes for relevant keywords, rank candidate skills, and analyze answers to early screening questions.



What this means for you

Your resume is increasingly being read by AI before a human ever sees it. But, if companies use AI to screen candidates, you should use AI to prepare for the screening.

Start with your resume. Upload your CV and the job description into a tool like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Ask it to:

Check whether your resume contains the key skills and keywords from the job post.
Suggest missing terms or phrasing.
Highlight where your experience could be made more specific or measurable.
This helps ensure your resume is “AI-friendly” before it enters an automated filter.

Next, prepare for screening interviews the same way. Ask AI:

What questions are likely for this role.
How strong your answers are.
How to improve clarity, structure, or impact.

In short, use AI as your rehearsal partner.
Here are a few practical prompts you can copy-paste to get started.


Prompt #1:

“Here is a job description:
[paste job description]

Here is my resume [attached].

Act as an AI resume screening system.
Tell me:

Which important keywords or skills from the job description are missing in my resume
Which parts of my resume should be clearer or more specific
How likely my resume is to pass an automated screening
Give practical suggestions only.”

Prompt #2:

“You are an AI screening recruiter for this role.
Based on this job description, list:

The 8 most likely screening questions
What a strong answer should include for each question

Job description: [paste]”
