Essential tools for scraping competitor content

For the second agent to produce useful insights, it needs two things:

Access to the top results on Google.
Access to the actual content inside those pages.



So we added two tools to the workflow:

Searchapi.io to fetch the top organic search results for any query.
Firecrawl to scrape those pages and extract their full content.

After comparing setup complexity and pricing, this combination felt like the most practical starting point.

Searchapi includes 100 free searches, which is perfect for early experimentation.
Firecrawl’s free tier wasn’t sufficient for proper testing, so we recommend starting with the €13/month plan and scaling up if needed.

Updating the workflow

This stage of the workflow begins with the spreadsheet generated by the previous agent.
It contains two key elements: Our article URL and the target keyword.



Next, a Google Document is automatically created.
This document will store the full content of our article, along with the content scraped from competing pages on Google, and will later be used as input for the analysis agent.

Once the document is ready, Firecrawl first scrapes our article and inserts its content into the document.
After that, the searchapi node searches Google for the top five organic results for the target keyword.

This triggers a loop where Firecrawl scrapes each competitor’s content, stores it in the document, and repeats the process five times until all articles are captured.



And now comes the fun part.
With a complete document ready for analysis, the agent can finally step in.
It reviews the content of the top five competing articles to identify shared patterns and winning elements.
Then it analyzes our own article, highlighting what we’re already doing well and where the gaps are.
From there, it produces clear, actionable recommendations and prioritizes them by impact: high, medium, and low.

This makes it much easier to focus on the changes that can actually move rankings.

Next steps

With the foundation now in place, a few important pieces are still missing.


We want to compare outputs across different LLMs (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude) to determine which model delivers the most actionable insights and should power the system going forward.

And while the scraping and comparison stage is now working well, everything ultimately depends on feeding it the right keywords.
That means refining the prompts in the first agent that analyzes Google Search Console data.

Right now, it focuses on keywords where we already get traffic but haven’t broken into the top five.
But we could also explore low-traffic pages outside the top ten or even run fresh keyword research to guide entirely new content creation.

You can expect us to explore all of this in the upcoming episodes.
