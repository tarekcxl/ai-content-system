Title: Where Google AI Overview pulls its answers from (Analysis of 100 citations)

According to a recent study by Ahrefs, Google AI Overviews have reduced clicks to top-ranking content by up to 58%. As a result, getting cited in these AI-generated answers is becoming increasingly important for maintaining content visibility.

While writing the last edition of this newsletter about Bing’s new webmaster AI feature, we noticed something interesting. As we checked the sources behind some of the data we were referencing, the cited text kept appearing near the top of the page.

After seeing this happen several times, it raised a question: was this just coincidence, or does the position of an answer on the page influence whether Google AI Overviews cite it?

To find out, we ran a small study. We analyzed 100 Google AI Overview citations to see where the cited snippets appeared within the source pages.

Key insights


AI Overviews favor early answers. 55% of citations come from the top 30% of the page.
Most citations appear before the midpoint. 79% are found before the 60% mark.
Bottom sections are less frequently cited, representing 21% of citations.

Do the same rules apply to ChatGPT?

Funnily enough, while we were working on this analysis, we came across a post by Kevin Indig that looked at the same question, but from the perspective of ChatGPT citations.

Kevin analyzed 1.2 million search results and 18,012 verified ChatGPT citations to understand where cited content tends to appear within a page.

The results show a very similar pattern.



According to the study, 44.2% of ChatGPT citations come from the first 30% of a document. 
After that point, the likelihood of being cited drops significantly, creating what Kevin describes as a “ski ramp” effect, where citations steadily decline as the page progresses.

Content placed at the very bottom of a page is rarely cited. Only 6.9% of citations come from the last 10% of a document.


What does this mean for you

The biggest takeaway from this analysis is simple: answer early.

If you want your content to be cited in AI Overviews, it's increasingly important to define the topic and explain the core idea near the beginning of the article.

Yes, storytelling and long introductions can make content engaging. But when it comes to AI citations, getting to the point early matters more. In other words, don’t bury the answer deep in the article.

At the same time, don’t ignore the bottom of the page either. A smaller portion of citations still come from deeper sections, often from FAQ blocks that address specific questions.
