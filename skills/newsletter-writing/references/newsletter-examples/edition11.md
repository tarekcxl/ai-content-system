Title: How to track your AI visibility with Bing (and what’s still missing)

For the past two years, tracking AI visibility and citations has largely been a black box.

Tools like Semrush and Ahrefs introduced ways to estimate AI visibility by monitoring search results and citations. But until recently, we didn’t have first-party data directly from the search engines themselves confirming when and how often our content was used inside AI-generated answers.

That changed a few weeks ago.

Bing Webmaster Tools introduced AI performance reporting, allowing website owners to see impressions and queries tied specifically to AI-generated answers. 


Naturally, there’s a lot of hype online.

We tested it ourselves. Here’s what it actually shows, what you can do with it today, and where it still falls short.

How it works



The new report focuses on citations when Bing’s AI retrieves and references your content to generate an answer.

Specifically, it shows:

Total citations: how often your pages were referenced in AI answers.
Cited pages: which URLs are most frequently used by AI.
Grounding queries: the types of queries Bing’s AI used to retrieve your content.
Visibility trends: how citation activity changes over time.

We’ve said this in previous editions: GEO or AEO isn’t a complete revolution, despite the hype around those terms. It’s more of a shift.

Yes, there are things you can tweak to increase your chances of being cited like strengthening your authority, earning mentions across the web, and creating highly structured, reference-worthy content. 

But the foundation remains the same: well-researched and high-quality content.

AI visibility is building on traditional SEO, not replacing it.

The missing piece of the puzzle

This is a strong first step for tracking AI visibility, especially since the data comes directly from the search engine itself.

But it’s still missing one critical piece: clicks.

Right now, the dashboard shows when your content is cited, but it doesn’t tell you whether those citations actually drive traffic. That makes it harder to measure real impact and even harder to know what to optimize.

For example, which cited pages actually bring visitors? Which topics encourage users to click through and learn more? And what changes could increase that likelihood?

Without click data, you can see where you’re visible, but not where you’re effective.

The feature has only been live for a few weeks, so hopefully Microsoft will expand and improve it in future updates.

Tracking AI visibility on Google and other LLMs

It’s also important to keep the context in mind: Bing represents a relatively small share of overall search usage compared to Google. 

That means what gets cited in Bing or Copilot doesn’t automatically reflect what’s happening across Google’s AI Overviews or other LLM-powered platforms.

For now, what we recommend is simple: export the grounding queries from this new report. These are actual queries your audience is using.



Take those queries and run them on Google, ChatGPT, and other LLMs. See if your content gets cited. If it doesn’t, check who does and analyze what they’ve done differently.



Of course, results can vary depending on the user and context, so it won’t be as accurate as getting the data directly from the source. But it gives you a valuable starting point. 

At the very least, you now have real queries connected to AI visibility and those same queries are likely being used across other search engines and AI platforms.

What about Google Search Console

We really hope this move by Microsoft encourages Google to do the same in Search Console, because that would be a real game changer.

Until that happens, you can use this trick to find AI-style prompts:

Go to GSC → Performance → Queries
Click Add filter → Query → Custom (regex)
Use a long-tail extractor like: ([^" "]*\s){7,}?



This will surface longer, problem-focused searches.
These often resemble the way people write prompts in ChatGPT and other LLMs.



AI visibility is finally becoming measurable, and that alone is a major shift. Use it to refine your content, strengthen your authority, and stay visible wherever your audience searches.
